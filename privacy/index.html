ThinkTrail

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 11, 2026

The short version
  • ThinkTrail records only while you're recording a session.
  • Your sessions are stored in your own browser. ThinkTrail has no account system.
  • When a session ends, it's analyzed with ThinkTrail's free credits unless you choose otherwise: the session goes to our server, which passes it to an AI and keeps none of it. You can switch to analysis on your own computer, where nothing leaves it.
  • Otherwise your data leaves your computer only when you send it: to an AI you pick, to Notion, or to Dodo Payments when you buy or activate Pro.
  • If you leave your email on this website to hear about new features, we keep that address and nothing else.
  • We don't sell your data, show ads, or use analytics or tracking.

This policy explains what the ThinkTrail browser extension ("ThinkTrail", "we") collects, where it goes, and the choices you have. It covers the extension and this website, thinktrailapp.github.io.

1. What ThinkTrail records

Nothing is recorded until you start a session, and recording stops when you pause or stop it. While a session is recording, ThinkTrail records the following about the tab you're using:

DataDetails
Your session goalThe text you write when you start a session. It can be empty.
Pages you visitThe page address, title and site, and when you arrived and left.
TimeTime on each page, time away from Chrome, and whether a tab was playing audio.
Page textA short excerpt (up to 600 characters) after 20 seconds on a page, and up to 4,000 characters of its main text after 30 seconds. ThinkTrail uses this to understand what a page was about and to write study notes. It never reads text from a page that has a password field.
Interaction countsHow many clicks, keystrokes and scrolls happened, how far you scrolled, and how far your pointer traveled. ThinkTrail counts these but never records what you typed, which keys you pressed, the contents of any form field, or where your pointer was.

On sites you add to the Excluded sites list in Settings, ThinkTrail records only the time spent there: no address, title or text.

2. Where your data is stored

Sessions, reports, notes, flashcards, your review history and settings are stored locally in your browser, in its extension storage. They aren't uploaded to us. The only exception is analysis with ThinkTrail's free credits (section 3), whose server keeps nothing from the session.

Sending to your notes app

If you connect a folder (such as an Obsidian vault), ThinkTrail writes Markdown files into a ThinkTrail subfolder there, on your own computer. Chrome asks your permission before it can write there.

If you connect Notion, ThinkTrail sends your analyzed sessions (titles, goals, times, summaries, activities, notes, source links and flashcards) to the Notion page you choose, using the integration secret you paste. The secret is stored only in your browser. Notion's privacy policy covers what's stored in your workspace. Disconnecting in Settings stops all sending; pages already in Notion stay there until you delete them.

If you use the optional Claude Code route (section 3), session summaries are also written to a folder on your own computer, ~/.thinktrail/sessions, so Claude Code can read them.

3. Analysis: you choose where it runs

Out of the box, ThinkTrail analyzes each session automatically when it ends, using ThinkTrail's free credits. Settings → Analysis says so, and is where you can choose another method, or turn automatic analysis off so nothing is analyzed until you press Analyze. Automatic analysis only ever uses the method chosen there (or, as a fallback, your own computer). Each option handles your data differently:

On this computer (Gemini Nano)

Analysis runs with the AI model built into Chrome, on your device. Your session data isn't sent anywhere. The first time, Chrome downloads the model from Google, and Google's terms for Chrome apply to that download.

Your own AI key

If you paste an API key from Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI or Google (Gemini), ThinkTrail sends the session summary, and for notes the text of the pages that served your goal, directly from your browser to that provider, using your key. Nothing passes through us. The key is stored only in your browser. The provider's terms and privacy policy apply, and you pay the provider for what you use.

ThinkTrail free credits (the default)

With free credits, ThinkTrail sends the session summary, and for notes the text of the pages that served your goal, to ThinkTrail's server, which runs with a cloud infrastructure provider. The server passes it to an AI model run by that provider and returns the answer to your browser. It doesn't store or log the content. To count free analyses it keeps only: a random id made by the extension for your install (not linked to your name or email), a short random id for each analyzed session, the number of sessions used this month, a one-way, salted fingerprint of your network (your IP address, or for IPv6 its network prefix), so new install ids don't mean new credits, and how much AI time each network and everyone used today. Monthly counts reset each month and daily ones each day. The server's logs record only sizes, timings and outcomes; the provider's automatic request logs, which can include IP addresses, are turned off. The provider processes the requests on our behalf, under a data processing agreement, and doesn't use them to train AI models.

A chat AI you already use

ThinkTrail puts the session summary and instructions on your clipboard, and you paste them into the AI service of your choice, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini. That service's privacy policy then applies to what you paste. ThinkTrail doesn't send anything itself.

Claude Code

If you set up Claude Code and press Send to Claude Code, the session summary is handed to a small program running on your computer. Claude Code reads it and sends it to Anthropic to produce the analysis, under Anthropic's terms and privacy policy.

Session summaries sent for analysis leave out query strings from page addresses, and pages from excluded sites appear without any detail.

4. ThinkTrail Pro and payments

ThinkTrail Pro is sold through Dodo Payments, which acts as the merchant of record. When you buy Pro, you enter your payment details and email address on Dodo's checkout page, never in ThinkTrail. Dodo's privacy policy covers that information. We receive the sale record from Dodo, including your email address, so we can support your purchase.

To turn Pro on and keep it working, the extension sends Dodo your license key and a device label such as "ThinkTrail on MacIntel". It does this when you activate a key, and then at most once a day while you use ThinkTrail, to check the key is still valid. Dodo's reply includes the email address on your purchase, which the extension stores locally to show which account Pro belongs to.

After checkout, Dodo emails you the license key, and you paste it into ThinkTrail yourself. The thank-you page Dodo sends you to doesn't read or show your key, and ThinkTrail runs nothing on this website.

5. This website

This website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log technical information such as your IP address when you visit, under GitHub's privacy statement. We don't add analytics, cookies or trackers to the site.

The new-features list

If you enter your email address in the waitlist form, it's sent to ThinkTrail's server, which stores it with the date you joined. That's all: it isn't linked to your ThinkTrail install, your sessions or your network, and the server's logs don't record it. We use it only to email you when ThinkTrail Pro, described on the form, is released. We don't share it with anyone except the service we use to send that email. Every email we send has a link that removes your address straight away, and you can also ask us to remove it (section 11). We delete the list once those emails have gone out.

6. What we don't do

  • We don't sell or rent your data, or share it with advertisers or data brokers.
  • We don't use your data for advertising, credit decisions or anything unrelated to ThinkTrail's purpose.
  • We don't run analytics or tracking in the extension.

ThinkTrail's use of information complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

7. Why ThinkTrail needs its permissions

  • Access to the sites you visit: to see which page you're on and measure time and reading while a session is recording. Chrome asks for it the first time you press Record, not when you install. It also lets ThinkTrail reach Notion if you connect it; if you connect Notion before recording, Chrome asks for access to Notion alone.
  • Scripting: to start and stop measuring on a page when recording starts and stops.
  • Idle: to notice when you've stepped away, so away time isn't counted as browsing.
  • Storage and unlimited storage: to keep your sessions in your browser.
  • Alarms: to pause and end sessions on time after you've been away.
  • Notifications: asked for when you turn nudges on, to nudge you, if you've turned nudges on, when you've spent a while on a distracting site during a session with a goal.
  • Favicon: to show site icons in your reports.
  • History (only if you use Import from history): to read the day you pick from Chrome's history, so ThinkTrail can show where that time went. It reads only that day and only this computer's visits, skips your excluded sites, and never changes or deletes your history. Imported sessions are stored in your browser like recorded ones, and analyzed only when you choose.
  • Offscreen: to run analysis in the background with Chrome's built-in AI or your own Claude key, which need a hidden page, after a session ends.

8. Your choices and control

  • See your data: every session is in History, and you can download a session's summary and notes from its report.
  • Delete a session: press Delete at the bottom of its report.
  • Delete everything: press Delete all sessions in Settings, or uninstall ThinkTrail, and Chrome removes all of its stored data. If you used the Claude Code route, also delete the ~/.thinktrail folder.
  • Keep sites out: add them to Excluded sites in Settings.
  • Pro on a computer: use Deactivate on this computer in Settings to remove your license key from that device.
  • Purchase records: to request access to or deletion of your purchase information, contact us (section 11) or Dodo Payments.

9. Children

ThinkTrail isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them.

10. Changes to this policy

If ThinkTrail starts handling data differently, for example by adding syncing to your phone, we'll update this page and change the date at the top before the change takes effect.

11. Contact

Questions about this policy or your data: open an issue on GitHub.
